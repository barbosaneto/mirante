# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Mirante contributors, maintainers, and community members commit to making
participation welcoming and free from harassment for everyone, regardless of
background, identity, experience, ability, appearance, nationality, or belief.

We value respectful disagreement, clear technical reasoning, empathy for
users, and patience with people learning the project.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, constructive, and specific.
- Assume good intent while addressing the actual impact of words and actions.
- Welcome questions and explain unfamiliar project context.
- Give and accept technical feedback without personal attacks.
- Credit other people's work and respect licenses and confidentiality.
- Protect private information, credentials, and security reports.
- Focus discussions on what benefits the project and its community.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, discrimination, or threats.
- Sexualized language or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Insults, trolling, sustained disruption, or deliberate humiliation.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.
- Any conduct that would be inappropriate in a professional community.

## Scope

This policy applies in project repositories, issue trackers, reviews,
discussions, events, and other spaces where someone represents the project.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns privately to the maintainers through the repository's
private contact mechanisms. Do not open a public issue containing sensitive
personal information. Reports will be handled as confidentially as practical;
information may be shared with only the people needed to assess and resolve the
matter.

Maintainers may edit or remove content and may warn, temporarily restrict, or
permanently exclude participants whose behavior violates this policy. They
will consider context, severity, pattern, impact, and whether the participant
takes responsibility for correcting the behavior.

Anyone directly involved in a report must recuse themselves from deciding its
outcome when another maintainer is available.
